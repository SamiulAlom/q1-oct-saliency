## 1. Working title
Do the heat maps hold? Faithfulness of saliency explanations across architectures and pathologies in retinal OCT classification.

Target: QPAIN 2027, Track 3, topic "AI in Biophotonics and Healthcare Diagnostics" (4-6 pages, IEEE template, double-blind, deadline 20 December 2026). Type: method-comparison (benchmark) paper; no new model is proposed. Reporting: CLAIM checklist items that apply to a retrospective public-data study, plus a reproducibility checklist.

## 2. Question
Retinal OCT classifiers are routinely published with Grad-CAM pictures as evidence that the model "looks at the lesion". Explanations on OCT have been scored before, but not in the way asked here (verified 2026-11-19, see section 12): a general benchmark includes the Kermany OCT data among nine datasets with three large models and reports no per-disease or patient-level analysis (LATEC, Klein 2024); one OCT study scores a single VGG16 on a binary task with 50 samples (Yavari 2026); one proposes a new model and notes that Grad-CAM++ faithfulness is class-dependent for that model (PhysioSpeck-Net, 2026).

- **Primary question.** For six named lightweight classifiers, two from each of three architecture families (convolutional, hybrid convolution-transformer, transformer), trained with one fixed protocol on the same patient- and duplicate-grouped split, does the faithfulness of saliency explanations differ between the family pairs? Inference is limited to these six models under this protocol; "family" is a label for a pair of models, not a claim about all models of that kind.
- **Secondary question.** Does faithfulness differ between pathologies (CNV, DME, drusen)?
- "Faithfulness" here means faithful to the model (removing the highlighted region changes the model's output). It does **not** mean clinically correct: the datasets have no lesion masks, so no claim about clinical correctness will be made.

Hypotheses (two-sided, stated before any result):
- H1: mean faithfulness differs between at least two architecture families.
- H2: mean faithfulness differs between at least two of the three disease classes.
Either a difference or no detectable difference is a reportable result.

Split (Kermany), built by notebook 01 before any training:
1. Ignore the dataset's own train/test folders (image- and subject-level overlap reported by Tampu 2022, doi:10.1038/s41597-022-01618-6).
2. Parse the patient identifier from each file name. Treat identifiers as global across class folders (reported by Jaman 2026, doi:10.5120/ijca8fee57096b16; to be confirmed by the audit).
3. Find near-duplicate images with a perceptual hash (64-bit pHash, Hamming distance 4 or less) across the whole dataset.
4. Build groups as connected components of "same patient OR near-duplicate". Each group goes whole into one partition.
5. Partition groups 70% train, 10% validation, 20% test, stratified by the group's majority class, seed 20261119. The split file (image, group, partition) is saved with its SHA-256 and never changed.
6. Training set cap, fixed now for compute reasons: at most 12 images per patient-class pair, chosen by sorted file name. Validation and test are not capped for accuracy.
7. Attribution test set: one image per test patient per class (first by sorted file name), then at most 250 patients per class chosen by seeded shuffle: at most 1,000 images. This list is fixed before any model is trained and is the same for every model, seed and method.
8. Checks written into notebook 01 and asserted on every later load: the file-name parser is tested on a sample from every class folder; group membership of every image is saved; no group appears in two partitions (zero patient crossings, zero duplicate crossings).
9. Unit of analysis: the group (connected component), which is one patient except where duplicate links join patients. A group that contributes images in more than one class is resampled as a whole.

The audit numbers (images, patients, shared identifiers, duplicate pairs, final partition sizes) are reported in the paper whatever they are. If fewer than 100 test patients remain in any class, that is reported and the class keeps all its patients.

OCTDL (exploratory): only DME and NORMAL images, after the same duplicate grouping, one image per patient. No training or tuning on OCTDL.

## 4. Models
Six public architectures with ImageNet-pretrained weights from `timm`, two per family, all under about 25 million parameters:
| Family | Models |
|---|---|
| Convolutional | EfficientNet-B0; MobileNetV3-Large |
| Hybrid | MobileViT-S; EfficientFormer-L1 |
| Transformer | DeiT-Tiny; DeiT-Small |

If a model cannot be loaded in the Colab environment, it is replaced by the closest model of the same family and the replacement is listed as a deviation in section 10.

Training, identical for all models: input 224 x 224 (square resize; grayscale replicated to 3 channels), AdamW, learning rate 3e-4 with cosine decay, weight decay 0.05, batch 64, 10 epochs, class-weighted cross-entropy, augmentation limited to horizontal flip and small random crop. Model selection: best validation balanced accuracy. No per-model tuning, so no model gets a larger tuning budget. Three seeds per model (11, 22, 33): 18 training runs. Package versions and the Colab image are recorded in the manifest.

Class weights are the inverse class frequencies of the capped training set, normalised to mean 1.

A model enters the faithfulness comparison only if its **validation** balanced accuracy is at least 0.85 on every seed (fixed now; the test set is not used for this decision). Excluded models are reported with their accuracy. If one model of a family is excluded, that family is represented by its remaining model and this is stated; if both are excluded, contrasts involving that family are reported as not estimable.

## 5. Explanation methods
Applied to the **true class**, on every image of the fixed attribution test set, for every model and seed, so that all models explain the same scans. Sensitivity analysis: the subset of images that all eligible models and seeds classify correctly, with the number retained per class reported.

Grad-CAM validity: the target layer and the reshape are fixed per model using validation images only. A Grad-CAM configuration is valid only if its maps have non-zero spatial gradients and are not constant on at least 95% of 200 validation images. A model with no valid configuration has Grad-CAM reported as "unavailable" and is left out of the Grad-CAM contrasts; it stays in the Integrated Gradients and occlusion contrasts. The shared grid equalises how images are perturbed, not the native resolution of each method, and the paper says so.

Methods:
| Method | Why |
|---|---|
| Grad-CAM | The method most used in OCT papers. Layer: last convolutional stage (convolutional and hybrid), last block's patch tokens reshaped to a grid (transformer). Layer names are fixed in the notebook before test data is used. |
| Integrated Gradients | Gradient-based and applicable to every family; 32 steps, black baseline. |
| Occlusion sensitivity | Model-agnostic reference; 16 x 16 patches, stride 16, blurred fill. |
| Random map (control) | A random saliency map per image: the chance level. |
| Edge map (control) | Sobel edges of the image: a model-independent map, to detect scores that reward any structure-following map. |

Every map is averaged onto the same 14 x 14 grid before scoring, so that methods with coarse and fine output are compared at equal resolution. Attention rollout is reported for the two transformers only, as a descriptive extra, not in the family comparison.

## 6. Outcomes
For each image, model, seed and method:
- **Deletion AUC**: grid cells are replaced by a Gaussian-blurred copy of the image in decreasing order of saliency, 14 cells per step; area under the curve of the predicted-class probability. Lower is more faithful.
- **Insertion AUC**: starting from the blurred image, cells are restored in the same order. Higher is more faithful.
- Scoring details, fixed now: blur is Gaussian with sigma 11 pixels on the 224 x 224 input; curves have 15 points (0 to 196 cells in steps of 14) including both end points; AUC by the trapezoid rule on a 0-1 x-axis; ties in saliency are broken by a seeded random order; the random-map control is the mean over 5 seeded random orders, the same 5 for every model, method and fill.
- **Primary outcome: faithfulness gap** = insertion AUC minus deletion AUC, minus the same quantity for the random map on the same image and model. Zero means no better than chance.
- Secondary: deletion and insertion AUC separately; the same with a mean-intensity fill instead of blur (sensitivity to the fill choice); the edge-map control; balanced accuracy and macro F1 per model.
- Sanity check (secondary): for one seed per model, Grad-CAM and Integrated Gradients are recomputed after the classifier head's weights are randomised; a method whose maps barely change (rank correlation above 0.9) is flagged as insensitive to the model.

## 7. Statistical analysis
- Unit: the group (section 3, item 9). Seeds are replicates of the model; per image, the outcome is averaged over the 3 seeds before testing.
- Resampling: one cluster bootstrap over groups (10,000 resamples, seed 20261119), in which each resample is applied jointly to all models, methods and classes, so every contrast uses the same resampled groups.
- **Primary analysis (H1).** For each of the three explanation methods, the mean faithfulness gap per family pair (average of its two models). Three pairwise contrasts per method: 9 tests. Each test is a centred, studentised bootstrap test of "difference = 0"; the 9 p-values are Holm-corrected together. Ordinary 95% bootstrap intervals are reported and labelled as pointwise (not multiplicity-adjusted). A contrast is called a difference when its Holm-adjusted p-value is below 0.05.
- **Secondary analysis (H2).** Per method, mean faithfulness gap in CNV, DME and DRUSEN, pooled over models; three pairwise contrasts per method from the same cluster bootstrap, 9 tests, Holm-corrected as a second family. NORMAL is reported descriptively only, because "the region that explains a normal scan" is not well defined (as Yavari 2026 also notes).
- Seed spread: the standard deviation over seeds is reported for every model; a family difference smaller than the within-model seed spread is described as not robust.
- Everything else (per-model results, OCTDL, fill sensitivity, controls, sanity check) is labelled exploratory and reported with intervals, without significance claims.
- No power calculation is possible before the audit fixes the number of test patients; the paper reports the smallest difference the final sample could detect, not "observed power".

## 8. Exploratory analyses (labelled as such in the paper)
- **Scanner shift.** The Kermany-trained models are applied unchanged to OCTDL DME and NORMAL images; faithfulness gap and accuracy are reported next to the Kermany values.
- **Speckle stability (conditional).** Only if the full-text reading of the PhysioSpeck-Net paper shows it does not already do this: multiplicative speckle noise at three stated strengths is added to test images; for images whose predicted class does not change, the rank correlation between the clean and noisy saliency maps is reported per family. The noise is a labelled perturbation used as a test method, never presented as observed data (integrity rule 1).

**Cut order if measured compute or time is short (fixed now):** first the OCTDL scanner-shift analysis, then the speckle analysis, then the cap of 250 patients per class is lowered to 150. Seeds, controls and the six models are not cut. Before the full attribution run, one T4 slice covering every model and method is timed and the compute plan is updated with measured figures.

## 9. What will not be claimed
- No claim to be a new general benchmark; LATEC is that. This is a focused study of one imaging domain.
- No claim about architecture families in general: only the six named models under the fixed training protocol.
- No claim that any explanation is clinically correct or useful to clinicians.
- No claim that one architecture is better for diagnosis.
- No claim beyond the two datasets and the image size used.
- Known weakness of deletion and insertion scores: removing regions produces images unlike the training data. The blur fill, the mean-fill sensitivity run and the random and edge controls are there to bound this; the limitation is stated in the paper.
